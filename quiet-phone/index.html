Green Grid, Silent Phone

A local visibility report can be entirely green while the business takes fewer calls than last year, and both things can be true at once. This is the full account of why: the twenty-four causes, how to measure every stage instead of only the first one, how to capture and read what actually happens on the phone, and the operational dashboard that puts it in front of the people who can act on it.

01

The eight stages, and the one that gets measured

Between a search happening and money arriving there are eight distinct stages. Each one can fail independently. A geo-grid measures the first — whether a pin appears on a map at a set of sampled coordinates — and reports it as the health of the whole chain.

This is the structural problem. Not dishonesty — a leading indicator adopted because the lagging ones were noisy, then quietly promoted to the only indicator. The grid is genuinely useful in the band where it discriminates. Above roughly eighty per cent presence it has no headroom left, so it cannot register a gain or a loss, and every subsequent report says the same thing regardless of what the business experiences.

A metric that is already green cannot show you improvement. Most local reporting is measuring the part that is already won.

The field observation this comes from

A five-location service firm measured across all its markets sat at 80–95% local-pack presence at average position 1.4–2.1 — a grid almost entirely green, in every office. In the same audit it lost or tied on organic in four of those five markets, and one office had 13 of 50 tracked queries with no top-three presence at all, invisible inside a healthy-looking average. The grid had no room left to show either the deficit or any fix.

03

Measuring local honestly

Keep the grid — it is a real instrument. Fix how it is parameterised, stop reporting the average, and put three free data sources alongside it that measure demand rather than presence.

Fix the grid first

A grid result is a function of parameters chosen by whoever ran it. Radius, point density, keyword set and competitor set all move the number more than most optimisation work does. Any grid used as evidence has to disclose them and hold them fixed.

ParameterThe flattering choiceThe honest choiceWhy it matters
Radius1–2 miles around the officeThe real service radius, set once and frozenA tight radius is green by construction; nobody drives past three competitors to reach you
Density5×5, re-run until it looks good7×7 or 9×9, fixed grid, fixed cadenceSparse grids move a lot on resampling alone; that movement gets reported as progress
KeywordsHead terms already wonMoney terms — service + modifier + intentdentist near me is not what a root-canal emergency types
StatisticAverage position, share of voiceFull distribution + % of points with no top-3Avg 1.4 hid 13 blind queries in the case above
BandReport all movement as gainOnly movement inside the discriminating bandAbove ~80% presence the instrument has no resolution
ComparisonYour grid, alone, over timeYour grid against a fixed competitor setShare is relative; everyone can rise in a shrinking market

The one metric to add immediately

Headroom. The percentage of grid points where you are not in the top three. It is the only part of the grid that can still move, so it is the only part worth optimising against — and unlike share of voice it goes to zero when the work is genuinely done, which tells you to stop spending on it.

Then measure demand, not presence

Three sources, all free, all closer to revenue than any rank measurement. These are what turn a visibility report into a demand report.

SourceWhat it gives youThe metric that matters
Business Profile performanceImpressions, calls, direction requests, website clicks, booking clicks — per location, per month, split by how people found youContact rate per 1,000 impressions. The bridge from visibility to demand. If the grid improves and this stays flat, the grid gain was not real.
Discovery vs. branded splitWhether people searched your name or searched the serviceDiscovery search volume. Branded search reflects reputation and other channels. Discovery is the only part local SEO can claim.
Search Console, per location pageImpressions, clicks and position for the organic listings the grid ignores entirelyClick-through on location and service pages. The map pack is one of several ways to be found, and often not the biggest.

Add competitor review velocity to the same sheet — reviews gained per month across the fixed competitor set. It is the single best predictor of whether a top-three pin gets chosen, and it moves slowly enough to forecast.

Report shape that survives scrutiny. One line per location per month: headroom, contact rate per 1,000 impressions, discovery share, review gap versus the competitor set, and calls answered. Five numbers. If a stage moved, one of them moves and names itself. Nobody has to interpret a heat map.
04

Capturing and reading the phone

In most local service businesses the phone is where revenue is decided, and it is the least instrumented surface in the company. Four layers: capture it, transcribe it, classify it, close the loop to outcome.

Layer 1 — Capture

Tracking numbers by source, not one number everywhere. Dynamic insertion on the website so the number swaps by referring channel; static numbers for the fixed surfaces.

SurfaceNumber typeAnswers the question
Business Profile, per locationStatic tracking number, primary slotHow many calls the map listing actually produces
Local Services Ads / paidPlatform-native, reconciledWhether paid is intercepting calls above the pin
Website, organic sessionsDynamic insertion, pooledWhich page and which query group produced the call
Website, paid sessionsDynamic insertion, separate poolKeeps paid and organic from claiming the same call
Print, vehicle, signage, referralStatic, one per sourceWhat offline is worth, which nobody otherwise knows

The mistake that costs rankings

Putting a tracking number on the Business Profile in place of the real one, and leaving the real one on the website and every citation. Inconsistent primary numbers across the web is a genuine local ranking risk. The supported pattern is tracking number as primary, the business's real number as additional — Google accepts this and it keeps the citation graph coherent. Verify it holds after every profile edit; it silently reverts.

Layer 2 — Transcribe

Record and transcribe every inbound call, subject to the consent rules in section 07. Transcription is the step that converts the phone from an opaque volume number into analysable text. Everything downstream depends on it.

Redact before anything leaves your control: names, dates of birth, card numbers, medical detail, case specifics. Redaction happens between transcription and classification, not after.

Layer 3 — Classify

This is where the value is, and where a language model earns its place. Every call gets scored on the same schema, so calls become countable rather than anecdotal. Fourteen fields, applied uniformly:

FieldValuesWhat it unlocks
Is it a lead?New inquiry · existing client · vendor · spam · wrong number · internalThe single biggest correction. Raw call volume routinely overstates real leads by 40–60%.
Service linePer business taxonomyWhich service the marketing actually produced, versus which one it was aimed at
Qualified?Geography · service fit · urgency · payer/insurance · capacitySeparates "the phone rang" from "the phone rang with someone we can serve"
OutcomeBooked · callback promised · declined by caller · declined by us · unresolvedThe conversion denominator nobody usually has
Answer latencySeconds to humanThe strongest single lever in the whole chain
Hold and transfer countIntegerExplains abandons that look like disinterest
Who answeredAgent / location / after-hours serviceCoaching signal, and routing faults across locations
Script adherencePer checkpointTurns intake training into something measurable
Failure reasonFree text → clustered taxonomyThe list of things to fix, ranked by how much they cost
Sentiment trajectoryStart → endFinds calls that were winnable and were lost in handling

The derived numbers that change the conversation

Qualified lead rate (qualified ÷ total calls), booked rate (booked ÷ qualified), missed-call rate and after-hours share, speed-to-answer distribution — the distribution, never the average, because a 12-second mean can hide a fifth of calls waiting over a minute. These four turn "we got 340 calls" into "we got 148 real leads, booked 61, and lost 44 to a phone nobody picked up."

Layer 4 — Close the loop

A booked appointment is not revenue. The chain only closes when the call record carries through to the system of record — practice management, case management, CRM — and comes back with what happened: showed, retained, completed, what it was worth.

Join on the tracking number plus timestamp, and where possible a lead ID written into the CRM at creation. Expect to reconcile: some percentage will never join cleanly, and the honest thing is to report matched and unmatched separately rather than quietly extrapolating.

Cost per signed case, by location, by service line, by source. Everything above exists to produce that one number defensibly.

05

One operational dashboard

The point is not another reporting layer. It is that every stage of the chain lands in one place, each metric names the stage it belongs to, and each role sees the part they can actually change.

What the AI layer does — six jobs, not a chat box

"Ask your data anything" produces confident answers nobody can audit. Narrow, named jobs with stated confidence produce work people act on.

JobWhat it doesGuardrail
1 · ClassifyScores every call, form and chat against the fixed schemaHuman-labelled sample each month to measure drift; publish the agreement rate
2 · AttributeJoins lead → source → outcome, with a confidence on each joinUnmatched stays unmatched. Never redistribute unknowns across known sources.
3 · DetectWatches each stage for breaks — answer rate, booked rate, headroom, review gapAlert on stage-level change, not on aggregate revenue, or everything is an alert
4 · ExplainWhen a number moves, names which stage moved and shows the calls or queries behind itEvery explanation links to the underlying records. No claim without a drill-down.
5 · BriefWritten summary per role, on the cadence that role operates atSays "nothing changed" when nothing changed. A brief that always finds something is noise.
6 · Track experimentsHolds live tests with baseline, cadence, success and kill criteriaKill criteria are set before launch and enforced automatically

What each team actually sees

Same data, five surfaces. Nobody gets a metric they cannot act on.

The design rule that makes it work

Every metric displays the stage it belongs to. Visibility metrics are labelled visibility. Capture metrics are labelled capture. It becomes structurally impossible to answer a revenue question with a visibility number, which is the failure the whole system exists to prevent.
06

Rollout, and what it costs

Sequenced so that each phase produces a usable answer on its own. If the engagement stops after phase one, phase one still paid for itself.

Phase 1 · Days 1–30 — Find out what is actually true

No optimisation. Instrumentation and a baseline, because every argument after this depends on having one.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60 — Fix capture before fixing visibility

Capture failures are cheaper to fix and pay back faster than any ranking work. A missed call is a lead you already paid for.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90 — Then spend on visibility, where there is headroom

Only now, and only against the headroom number, so the spend goes where the instrument can still register it.

Rough cost shape

Call tracking runs per number plus per minute — for a five-location business with source-level numbers, low hundreds a month. Transcription and classification are cents per call at these volumes. Grid measurement is per scan, so it is a function of how many keywords and locations, and the honest protocol above costs more than the flattering one because it runs more points. The dominant cost is none of these — it is the integration into the system of record, which is where the schedule risk sits and where a project that skips discovery goes wrong.
07

Consent, privacy and the things that bite

Recording phone calls in service businesses is a regulated activity, and the regulation varies by where the caller is, not where the business is. This section is the part most vendors skip and most complaints come from.

  • Two-party consent. Several US states require every party to consent to a recording. Where the caller's location is unknown or the business serves multiple states, the safe rule is an announcement on every call and consent before recording begins. Verify current requirements with counsel for the specific states involved — this is not a settled thing to take from a web page, including this one.
  • Health information. If callers can disclose health details, the recording, the transcript and every downstream processor are handling protected information. That requires appropriate agreements with each vendor in the chain, including the transcription and classification services. Not every AI service will sign one.
  • Legal intake. Calls to a law firm may carry privilege from the first sentence. Recording, storage and third-party processing need to be cleared against the applicable professional conduct rules before anything is switched on.
  • Redaction before processing. Strip identifiers between transcription and classification. The classification schema above is deliberately built so it can be scored from a redacted transcript — none of the fourteen fields need a name.
  • Retention. Set a deletion schedule and enforce it. Recordings accumulate silently and become a liability rather than an asset once their analytical value has been extracted.
  • Staff. People whose calls are scored need to know the scoring exists, what it measures and what it is used for. Introduced as coaching it improves performance; discovered as surveillance it destroys the front desk, and the front desk is the asset.
Not legal advice. The above sets out where the risk sits so it can be raised early with someone qualified to answer it. Consent, privilege and health-information rules change and are jurisdiction-specific. Get them cleared before the first recording, not after.
